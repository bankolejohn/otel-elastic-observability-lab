## What actually happened
`slow` chaos mode adds `SELECT pg_sleep(2)` to every reservation. Orders succeeded, so there were no errors.

## How it showed up in telemetry
Across the drill window, 314 orders: **0 http 5xx, 0 error logs.** Availability SLI read 100%. Per-minute p95 of successful orders was ~4,100-4,300 ms (normal: ~100-200 ms).

Trace `ed365415dce7e57ae0fedb38ed84fc48` (whole request 4,946 ms vs a typical ~97 ms):

| span | ms | typical |
|---|---|---|
| `api-gateway` `POST /api/orders` (root) | 4,946 | ~97 |
| `inventory-service` `reserve_stock` | 3,885 | ~5 |
| `inventory-service` `SELECT appdb` (`SELECT pg_sleep(2)`) | 2,004 | ~1 |
| `inventory-service` `connect` | 1,870 | ~1 |

`reserve_stock` 3,885 ≈ 2,004 (the sleep) + 1,870 (`connect`). The `connect` time is most likely **queueing for a pooled connection**: the drill sends 6 concurrent orders against a pool of 5, and each sleeping reservation holds its connection for 2s. So the injected fault caused a second, knock-on delay of its own. The responder's "led to other issues" was correct.

## What worked
- The **latency alert caught what nothing else could** (see below).
- A baseline table of typical span durations made "slow" a comparison instead of a guess.
- `db.statement` on the database span named the cause directly.

## What was slow or misleading
- **The root span is always the slowest.** The responder first reported `api-gateway` (4,946 ms); the fix is to peel layers until the time stops shrinking, and compare each span to its typical duration.
- **`NOW() - 5 minutes` returns nothing once the incident has aged out.** "No results" can mean a wrong time window, not "no problem". Use fixed UTC windows when investigating after the fact.
- The responder asked "how do I know what's slow without a threshold?" — the answer (compare to a healthy baseline, use the SLO for the whole request, think in proportions) deserves to be a permanent dashboard panel.
